How this works

Method

What has to be true before a sentence appears on this site, and what happens to the things that are not.

One fact, one source

Nothing is written about a person and then sourced afterwards. It runs the other way. An editor records a source: a page, a register, a journal record, a patent, or a questionnaire the subject filled in, along with the date they actually looked at it. Then they record what that source says, one fact at a time, in its own words as well as in plain words.

A profile is assembled only from those facts. Each sentence declares which of them it rests on, and the software refuses to publish a sentence that declares nothing.

Three standings, and what each one means

Verified means an editor checked the claim against the primary source itself. Reported means a secondary source says so and nobody has gone back to the original; it can be published, and the editor sees a warning while they work. Disputed means two sources disagree.

A disputed claim is recorded, with a note saying what the disagreement is, and it is never published. Picking the more flattering of two contradictory sources is how a profile becomes fiction, so the software will not let anyone do it.

A source with no date is not a source

Every source carries the date an editor read it. Nothing can be cited from a source that has no such date, because a claim you cannot go back and check is an assertion wearing a citation as a costume.

Numbers do not drift

The most common failure in written biography is not invention, it is drift: a source saying more than forty five years becomes nearly fifty. Every figure in a published sentence has to appear in the source that sentence cites. If it does not, the sentence does not pass.

Consent, per purpose

Publishing a profile, issuing a release in someone's name, generating a likeness of them, and pointing a public contact form at their address are four separate permissions. Each is recorded with the channel it came through and the evidence for it. Consent relayed by a third party is recorded as exactly that, because it is weaker evidence than consent from the person themselves.

Language we do not use

Profiles do not congratulate anyone on appearing here. A publisher congratulating a subject on the publisher's own listing is advertising, not recognition. Unsourceable praise is rejected outright: prestigious, world renowned, the finest in the field. If a compliment cannot be traced to a source, it is not a fact and it does not appear.

Nothing is written by a machine alone

Drafting can be assisted, but an assisted draft is checked against the same rules as a typed one, and it is not published until a person who did not write it approves it. Where an image was generated rather than photographed, the page says so beside the image.

Corrections

A correction is checked against the original source, not against who is asking. If the source was wrong, the entry changes and the profile changes with it. If the source was right, we say so and leave it.

Recognition

Held to the standard above.

Recognition here means a profile prepared exactly this way: sourced, dated, reviewed by a second editor and correctable for as long as it stands.
