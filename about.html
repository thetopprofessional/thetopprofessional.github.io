About The Top Professional

Professional achievement deserves authoritative presentation.

The Top Professional publishes profiles of accomplished practitioners in which every statement is recorded against a dated source. On a wide screen the source is printed in the margin beside the sentence it supports, so a reader can tell where a claim came from without leaving the page.

Why we exist

Anyone will publish a flattering paragraph.

There is no shortage of places that will print a few admiring sentences about a professional. What is missing is one where a reader can tell, without leaving the page, where each claim came from and when somebody last checked it.

That gap is the whole reason for this publication. A profile here is assembled from recorded facts, each one tied to a source with the date an editor read it, and the software refuses to publish a sentence that rests on nothing.

It makes the profiles shorter. Plenty of true and interesting things about a person cannot appear, because nobody has produced a source for them. A profile that looks thin usually means the evidence is thin, and we would rather show that than pad it.

01

The standard

Evidence before adjectives.

Four rules decide whether a sentence is allowed onto this site. They are enforced by the software, not by good intentions.

  • Source

    Every published claim is tied to a source. A sentence that declares no source does not publish.

  • Date

    Sources carry the date an editor actually read them. A claim you cannot go back and check is not evidence.

  • Review

    Profiles are reviewed before publication by somebody who did not write them.

  • Corrections

    A correction is checked against the underlying source, not against who is asking.

Boundaries

What this is not.

  • Not a ranking

    Nothing here is ordered by merit. Being listed does not imply that somebody is better than anybody who is not.

  • Not an awarding body

    No prize is given and no honour is conferred. The publication records standing, it does not confer it.

  • Not a certification or licensing authority

    We do not certify, accredit or license anyone, and nothing here substitutes for a register that does.

02

Process

How a profile is made.

  1. 01

    Source

    An editor records a source and the date they read it: a register, a journal record, a patent, an employer page, or a questionnaire the subject completed.

  2. 02

    Research

    What that source says is recorded one fact at a time, in the source wording as well as in plain words, with its standing marked.

  3. 03

    Editorial review

    A draft is assembled only from those facts. Every sentence declares which it rests on, and a second reader approves it.

  4. 04

    Publication

    The profile publishes with its sources numbered and dated beneath it, and printed in the margin on a wide screen.

  5. 05

    Correction

    A correction goes back to the source. If the source was wrong the entry changes and the profile changes with it.

Who writes it

The subject does not control the wording.

Profiles are prepared by editorial staff from recorded sources, and approved by somebody who did not write them.

Subjects can supply information, correct what is wrong, and withdraw their consent at any time. They do not get to approve the wording, and they cannot ask for a flattering line that no source supports.

That distinction is the point of the thing. A profile the subject writes is a biography. A profile the subject can check but not dictate is a record.

Recognition

Professional recognition, presented properly.

Recognition here means a profile prepared to the standard above and published under this masthead: sourced, dated, reviewed by a second editor, and correctable. It is not a prize, and it is not a position in a league table.
